## Current official-source checks

- Smith.ai AI Receptionist: https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist — Free 25 calls; Pro $150/75 calls; Enterprise $500/300 calls. AI-first offer with described live-receptionist escalation.
- Smith.ai Virtual Receptionists: https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists — separate human-first offer. Starter $300/30 calls; additional calls $11.50. Appointment booking is listed as $1.50/call on that human-service pricing page.
- Ruby: https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/ — $250/50 minutes, $395/100, $720/200, $1,725/500. Live-receptionist positioning and advertised AI enhancements; no setup/activation fee on the cited page.
- Rosie: https://heyrosie.com/pricing — $49/250 minutes Professional, $149/1,000 Scale, $299/2,000 Growth. Distinguish appointment links on the entry offer from direct booking and warm/live transfers on Scale.
- Goodcall: https://www.goodcall.com/pricing — monthly per-agent $79/100 unique customers, $129/250, $249/500; $0.50 per extra unique customer; unlimited minutes/tokens. Unique customers are not calls or minutes.
- AnswerConnect Canada: https://www.answerconnect.com/ca/ — CAD $325/100 minutes plus CAD $75 setup; CAD $425/300 minutes without setup. Canadian pricing is explicitly identified.
- Dialzara: https://dialzara.com/pricing — direct retrieval failed. The indexed official result listed $29/60 minutes and $99/220, with $0.48/$0.45 overage; its crawl was approximately three months old. The visible article explicitly says these figures require reconfirmation. They are not represented as a freshly fetched current tariff.

Vendor statements establish advertised offers, not successful tests of a particular contractor workflow. Missing documentation is not proof a feature is impossible. Recommendations and the grouping of alternatives are Off The Clock editorial judgments.

## Changes from the original drafts

1. The Smith.ai comparison now distinguishes the AI Receptionist from the separate human-first Virtual Receptionists product. The original broad brand-level AI-first statement was too imprecise.
2. Ruby is described as human-first, not AI-free: its official page also describes AI enhancements.
3. Pricing includes its unit and relevant product. Calls, receptionist minutes and unique customers are not normalized with invented assumptions. Canadian AnswerConnect amounts are labeled CAD; no currency conversions or manufactured savings are used.
4. Rosie appointment links are not described as equivalent to direct calendar booking. Goodcall billing is identified as per-agent and unique-customer based.
5. The six original Ruby-alternative names are retained, but grouped by buyer need rather than presented as universal winners. Dialzara's retrieval limitation is visible, not hidden in internal notes.
6. The original full comparison hub is reduced to the two complete guides delivered in this checkpoint. Other planned pages are not represented by broken links or placeholder cards.
7. The review build does not treat the draft's Off The Clock prices, live demo or signup destinations as verified live checkout or operator behavior. Calls to action open the existing functional local V2 tools instead. Real product CTAs require separate publication approval.
8. The new resources hub and downloadable five-call checklist connect existing materials. They introduce no engine behavior, lead delivery, account creation, analytics or provider integration.
